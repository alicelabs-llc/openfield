THE PITCH

Simulation is the widest genre in games.

It's the breakout hit that has you optimizing a supply chain you invented for yourself. It's the management game you lose a weekend to. It's the tycoon loop, the survival colony, the vehicle you learn to actually fly.

All of it counts here. Build something that simulates a system — real, invented, serious, or ridiculous.

For six weeks, make a simulation game. The best ones win cash, get feedback from working industry veterans, and demo live on a stream that plays to both the jam community and a room of developers, studios, and educators.

Open to developers worldwide.

THEME

Theme: SIMULATION

Join the kickoff stream for design guidance and inspiration.

Your game needs to simulate a system of some kind. Beyond that, the field is wide open: Management, tycoon, colony, survival, vehicle, life sim, immersive sim, sandbox systems, physics toys.

JUDGING

Community rating (November 5–10) — decides the top 5

All participants rate on five criteria. Each is written to work across the full range of simulation, from a chaotic tycoon loop to a meticulous systems model.

CriterionWhat it asks
Systems Does the simulation hold together? Do player actions produce meaningful consequences and readable feedback? Do the mechanics interact in ways that create emergent situations?
Engagement Does it hold your attention and reward you for paying attention? Is it fun? Do you want another run?
Creativity Is the idea original? Does it feel distinct? A fresh premise, an unusual mechanic, an unexpected tone.
Theme Execution Does it interpret the jam theme well?
Presentation Art, sound, clarity, and overall polish. Does it feel good to be in?

Fidelity to reality is not a criterion. An abstracted, deliberately gamey system can score full marks on Systems if it's coherent and generative. A photorealistic model can score poorly if nothing you do matters.

Expert judging (November 11–14) — decides placement

HOW TO ENTER

You can join the jam and add team members at any time up to the submission deadline on November 4. After that, entries and teams are locked and rating begins — no late entries, and no members added after the fact.

  1. Click Join jam on this page.
  2. Join the Game Gauntlet Discord: https://discord.gg/DPd4n2MsU8. This is required, not optional, and it applies to every person on the team — not just whoever submits. Reviews, mentorship, and finalist coordination all happen there.
  3. Build your game between September 23 and November 4.
  4. Submit on itch.io before the deadline, including your team roster, and make sure everyone on the team has joined the server https://discord.gg/DPd4n2MsU8
  5. Play and rate at least 3 other entries during the rating window.

Why Discord is required: after you submit, we add you to a private review channel for your entry. That's where you'll get community feedback and expert industry review. If we can't match your submission to a Discord account, we can't get you into that channel — so the Discord usernames on your team roster have to match the accounts that joined the server.

Team size: solo, or up to 5 people. Everyone who worked on the game must be listed on the final submission, and every team member must be in the Discord.

SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS

Every submission must include:

  1. A playable build — Windows .exe or browser. Browser builds get the most plays and ratings, and travel best internationally.
  2. Title and description in English, including how your game relates to the theme. In-game language is up to you; the store page description needs to be in English so judges and raters can evaluate it.
  3. Controls and instructions
  4. A one-line statement of what your game is going for — what it simulates, and who it's for. Raters use this to judge your game on its own terms. (See "How to rate" below.)
  5. Your Discord username — must match the account that joined our server
  6. Credits for all third-party assets
  7. AI content disclosure (see below)

Original work

The core design, code, and content of your game must be created during the jam period, September 23 – November 4.

Permitted, as long as they're credited:

  • Purchased, licensed, or free asset packs
  • Your own pre-existing code libraries, frameworks, and tools
  • Game engines, middleware, and plugins
  • Stock music and sound effects

Not permitted:

  • A project you started before September 23, reskinned or extended for this jam
  • Someone else's game, or work you don't hold the rights to

Attestation. Every submission requires a signed attestation confirming the above (see the submission form). Submitting a false attestation is grounds for disqualification and forfeiture of any prize — including after results are announced.

You keep everything. Games and all underlying IP remain fully owned by the participants who made them.


AI DISCLOSURE / THIRD PARTY ASSETS

AI-generated art, code, music, and text are permitted. You must disclose them, aligned with Steam's requirements — this keeps your game shippable later.

Disclose:

  • Whether your game uses AI-generated content
  • Which types: art, code, music, text/dialogue

You must hold the rights or permission to use everything in your game, AI-generated content and third-party assets included. That means you created it, or you licensed it for personal or commercial use.


HOW TO RATE

This jam covers a wide range — a chaotic tycoon loop and a meticulous systems model can both be excellent, and both are welcome here.

Rate each game against what it's trying to do, not against what you'd have made.

Every submission includes a one-line statement of intent. Read it first. A tense survival colony isn’t trying to be a laugh, and a physics toy isn’t trying to be accurate. Ask whether the game achieves what it set out to achieve, then rate accordingly.


RATING PERIOD

Rating runs November 5–10, closing Tuesday, November 10 at 2:00 PM ET / 8:00 PM CET (19:00 UTC). Every participant must rate at least 3 entries — teams that don't rate are ineligible for prizes. Three is the minimum, not the target: the more entries you rate, the fairer the top 5 is for everyone.

Do not solicit ratings or coordinate vote-trading. Entries that do will be disqualified.

Updating your build during rating

Uploads stay open during the rating period, but only for one reason: fixing game-breaking bugs. Crashes, softlocks, builds that won't launch, save corruption.

If you push a fix, post what you changed in your submission's devlog or in the jam community thread. Keep it short — one line is fine.

Not permitted during rating:

  • New content, levels, or features
  • Balance or difficulty changes
  • Art, audio, or UI polish

Everyone is rated on the same six weeks of work. Fixing a crash keeps that fair; shipping improvements while people are voting does not. Undisclosed changes are grounds for disqualification, and we check upload timestamps against devlogs before finalist builds go to the judges.

Avoid the problem entirely: browser builds are the least likely to strand you with a launch failure you never saw on your own machine.

FINALISTS

The top 5 are announced Wednesday, November 11. If you're in, two things happen.

1. Submit a 1–2 minute video — due before the panel. Introduce your team, describe your game, show gameplay. Due Friday, November 13 at 2:00 PM ET / 8:00 PM CET (19:00 UTC).

2. Show up and demo live. This is the one that matters.

The Judging Panel + Awards is a livestreamed online call on Saturday, November 14 at 2:00 PM ET / 8:00 PM CET (19:00 UTC). Finalists demo their game to the judges live and answer questions. Results are revealed on the stream.

Your video is the fallback if your team genuinely can't make the call — it gets shown in your place. But be clear about the tradeoff: in our experience, judges weigh live attendance heavily. A team that demos its own game and fields questions gives the panel far more to score than a recording can. If you can make the call, make the call.

Everything is online — no travel, ever. Wherever you are in the world, you join the same stream. We coordinate over Discord.

The awards video and the finalist games then screen at the Game Gauntlet Game Night x Vital Spaces on Saturday, November 21 (venue TBA). Your game reaches the online jam community on the 14th and an in-person audience on the 21st — without you going anywhere.
