I'm building a tool I'm not sure anyone needs.

by woasihab

A while ago I started hacking on Unwrap, a local-first explorer for messy data exports, archives, JSON, CSV, spreadsheets, images, documents, and whatever else people throw at it.

The idea was to stop building a separate viewer for every export format and instead figure out what's in the data automatically.

Turns out that's a pretty ridiculous problem to solve.

Every new dataset comes with its own weirdness. Fixing the heuristics for one export sometimes makes another worse. And the more I build, the more I understand why there aren't many tools like this.

I'm not even sure there are enough potential users to justify the effort.

But I really enjoy working on it, which makes it surprisingly hard to stop.

Anyone else building something that's probably too complicated for the number of people who might actually use it?

    There's a cheap test hiding in your own description. People who need something like Unwrap have usually paid for the problem already, just not with money: someone wrote a throwaway script for an export, or lost an afternoon untangling one in a spreadsheet. Instead of asking "would you use this," find ten people who did that recently and ask what it cost them in hours and whether they'd hand the job to a tool. Enjoying the work is a fine reason to keep building. A repeat user is the reason to keep building on purpose.
