Welcome!

Bezi Jam 14 is here. Four days, $400+ in prizes, and a theme that comes in two halves. This jam has two challenges: build a game around the theme, and show us your best Bezi fan art.

Both halves of the theme are now revealed, and so is the fan art prompt. Build your game with whatever engine and tools you like.

We run these jams to support game developers and to promote good development habits. We are committed to contributing to the game dev community when and where we can, and a jam is one of our favorite ways to do that. Whether this is your first jam or your fiftieth, you are welcome here.

He sees you when you are sleeping. He knows when you are awake. And he is already on his way.

🔓 The second half is unsealed.

Both themes are live, along with this jam's fan art prompt. Read them together: the second theme changes how you read the first. Head down to the Theme section for the full brief, and bring your questions to our Discord.

About Bezi

A bit about who we are. We believe AI will never replace people. Bezi is a productivity tool, built to take the friction out of the work so you can spend more of your time on the creative decisions only you can make. It is made for game developers by game developers, by a team that has shipped games and knows where the hours really go.

In practice, Bezi is a development assistant that integrates directly into your game engine, indexing your project to provide context-aware help. Whether you are debugging, generating shaders, building editor tools, or prototyping mechanics, it works from your actual project structure and coding style rather than generic answers. You can download Bezi here, or visit our itch.io page.

Using Bezi is optional. We would love for you to try it and tell us how it fits your workflow, but it is not required to enter or to win.

Theme

Theme One of Two

SOMETHING WICKED

This season, make us afraid of the dark again

Halloween is the theme, and the whole spectrum is yours: spooky, scary, tense, or genuinely frightening. Atmosphere counts as much as action, and a good scare always beats a big one.

💡 Ways to Read the Theme
Be the monster
Flip the lens. Play as the thing under the bed and let the humans be the problem 		The place is wrong
A house, a ship, a station that should be safe and is not. The setting is the antagonist
Outrun it
Something is hunting you. Manage the little you have, stay hidden, and do not get caught 		Dread over gore
The scariest thing is the one you never quite see. Sound, silence, and the unknown
The ritual
Curses, summonings, things you should not have read aloud. Rules that come with a price 		Cozy and spooky
Not every scare is grim. Charming, playful, and just a little bit haunted

Just starting points, not a menu. Twist them, combine them, or ignore them. If it fits the theme, it counts.

Theme Two of Two

🔓 THEME TWO

ANYTHING BUT HALLOWEEN

"The monster was never in the haunted house."

Something Wicked still stands, but leave the pumpkins at the door. Your game has to be wicked, tense, or frightening without leaning on Halloween. No jack-o'-lanterns, no trick-or-treating, no October. Find the wicked somewhere it doesn't belong: a sunny suburb, a corporate office, a kids' cartoon, a cozy café, a spaceship, a birthday party, a holiday morning.

And one more thing: something in your game is lying.

🤥 The Lie

Somewhere in your game, something isn't telling the truth. It could be the narrator, the UI, an NPC, the map, the rules, or the player's own eyes. It can run through the whole game or show up in a single moment. It only works if the player eventually finds out, or at least starts to suspect.

🚪 Ways In
The friendly face
The helpful guide, mascot, or assistant isn't on your side 		The interface lies
The health bar, map, timer, or tutorial is giving you bad information
Picture perfect
Everything looks fine. It isn't 		Unreliable you
The player character is the one hiding something
The rules change
What you learned in level one stops being true 		Too wholesome
Bright, cheerful, cozy, and deeply wrong
🎃 What Counts as "Halloween"?
Off the table
Jack-o'-lanterns, trick-or-treating, costumes, witches, sheet ghosts, graveyards, haunted houses, and the classic monster lineup (vampires, werewolves, mummies, zombies). Bats and black cats used as spooky decoration also count 		Fair game
Any other holiday, plus dread, tension, horror, darkness, and monsters you invent yourself. A sinister Christmas, a Valentine's Day gone wrong, a Thanksgiving dinner nobody leaves: go for it

Using classic Halloween imagery won't disqualify you, but it will cost you on Theme Interpretation.

Someone loves you more than anything in this world. They are never, ever going to let you go.

Prizes

All winners receive cash prizes, and the pool is $400+. The main game award is decided in two rounds: community voting narrows the field to a Top 30, then the Bezi team plays every one of those finalists and selects the winners. Best Devlog and Best Fan Art are chosen by the Bezi team.

🏆 Track 1: Best in Show

Open to all participants. Decided in two rounds: community voting sets the Top 30, then Bezi team judging decides places 1 through 3. Judging takes a few days, and winners are announced the Friday after community voting ends (November 6).

🥉 3rd Place $50

📝 Track 2: Best Devlog

Judged by the Bezi team. Open to all participants who post a devlog in #bezi-showcase on our Discord.

We frame this prize as a communication exercise. Knowing how to explain what you built and why you made the choices you made is a real skill, and it is one of the most valuable things you can show in a portfolio. A clear devlog tells a studio, a teammate, or a future collaborator how you think. That is what we are looking to reward here.

📝 Best Devlog (Bezi Team Judged)
🏆 Winner $50

Focus on your game first. The devlog is meant to be written during the voting period after the jam ends, not during the four days you are building. Finish your game, then tell the story of how you made it.

Post your devlog in #bezi-showcase on our Discord with the Bezi Jam tag. Tell us about your game, how you built it, and the thinking behind your decisions. Screenshots, GIFs, and video help bring it to life.

📋 Devlog Format
Game Name and a one-liner describing your game in one sentence
Play It with a link to your submission page
Screenshots / Video showing gameplay
About the Game sharing what players do and what makes it interesting
Built With listing your engine, tools, plugins, or any notable tech
How You Made It covering the decisions behind your design and why you made them, the problems you ran into, and how you solved them. If you used Bezi, tell us what role it played and any tips you discovered along the way

Voting & Judging Criteria

Round 1 runs on community voting, and we use Jamlytics to help keep results fair and transparent. Participants enter a rating queue of 5. You will rate 5 random submissions before rating other entries. Community voting determines the Top 30 finalists.

In Round 2, the Bezi team plays all 30 finalists and scores them on the same criteria below to decide the winners. Whichever round you are in, the criteria are the same.

Please vote with integrity and take time to play each game. We include an extended voting period so you can give each entry proper attention.

Each category is rated from 1 to 5 stars (1 = weak, 5 = outstanding).
