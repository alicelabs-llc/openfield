What you build

The game is yours. Solo or team, any engine, any genre, any platform. Unity, Godot, Unreal, Phaser, Three.js, RPG Maker, GB Studio, plain HTML, whatever you already know.

The only requirement is that DreamLayer is meaningfully used somewhere in your game's visual process. That is deliberately broad. Concept art that shaped the direction counts. So does a single hero character you built the whole game around.

No theme is announced in advance. Make what you want to make.

What DreamLayer is good at

DreamLayer is an AI agent for creative work. You describe what you want, it picks the right image model for the job and handles the orchestration. For a game jam, the things it does well:

Character consistency. This is the one worth knowing about. Generate a character once, approve it, then use that image as the reference for every later image. The same character carries across a portrait, an action pose, a different outfit, a different scene.

Turnarounds and reference sheets. Front, side and back views of the same character, holding one identity. Useful if you need a character to look right from more than one angle.

Iterative editing. Change one thing and keep the rest. "Same character, now with a torch." "Same scene, now at night." "Keep the pose, change the armour."

Environments, items, UI and textures. Backgrounds, props, icons, tilesets, title screens.

A worked example

Here is the pattern most people land on:

  1. Describe your main character in one prompt. Be specific about the parts that must stay fixed: hair, clothing, one recognisable item.
  2. Generate until you have one you like. This is your reference.
  3. Attach that image and describe the next thing you need. "Keep this exact character. Show them mid jump, side view, plain background."
  4. Repeat for each asset. Every generation points back at the same approved reference, so the character stays the same.
  5. That loop is the whole trick. Most of the work is in step 2, getting one good reference you are happy to build on.

Submitting

Tell us what you built, who worked on it, and how DreamLayer was used.

Include screenshots or gameplay footage, and briefly show or explain which assets came from DreamLayer. A before and after, or a shot of your reference character next to where it ended up, is ideal.

Your game must be playable in some form. Browser builds get more plays and more votes than downloads, so use one if you can.

Judging criteria

  • Voting is open to everyone who submitted. Four criteria, equally weighted:
  • Gameplay & Fun. How enjoyable and engaging is the game?
  • Visual World & Art Direction. How well do the characters, environments and assets come together into a distinctive world?
  • Creative Use of DreamLayer. How creatively and effectively was DreamLayer used to create assets for the game?
  • Originality. How unique or unexpected is the game concept and execution?

Note that "Creative Use of DreamLayer" is one criterion of four, not the main one. We want good games that DreamLayer helped make, not tech demos.

Community

The jam community tab is open from today. Use it to find teammates, share generations, ask how someone got a particular style, and post progress.

Submissions stay visible during the jam. Seeing what other people are making tends to create momentum rather than nerves.

Good to know before you start

Being straight about this so nobody wastes jam hours:

  • DreamLayer does not snap to a pixel grid. Export resizing uses smooth resampling, so a 32x32 export does not automatically become clean pixel art. If your game needs grid-perfect pixels you will be cleaning up by hand or using a dedicated pixel tool.
  • It does not export .aseprite files with separate layers, and it does not produce coordinated 8-direction sets.
  • It is an image tool. No audio, no code, no 3D models.

Questions

  • Do I need to pay for anything? No. Credits are free for participants and there is no card required.
  • How many credits do I get? 100 API credits per participant, available through the API, CLI and MCP. These are separate from browser App credits. Claim yours here: https://forms.gle/f7GoK78bp9kEKzck7. It should be enough to build a full set of assets for a jam game. 
  • Can I use other AI tools as well? Yes. Use whatever you like alongside it.
  • Can I use art I made by hand? Yes, and please do. Mixing is encouraged.
  • Do I own what I make? Output rights go to you under the DreamLayer terms, subject to applicable law and the underlying provider terms.
  • Can I work in a team? Yes, any size. List your teammates on the submission.
  • Do I have to start from scratch? Yes, the game itself should be made during the jam. Assets and prototyping you did earlier are fine.
  • What if I do not finish? Submit anyway. An unfinished thing that gets played beats a perfect thing nobody sees.

From prompts to playable worlds. Good luck. 🎮
