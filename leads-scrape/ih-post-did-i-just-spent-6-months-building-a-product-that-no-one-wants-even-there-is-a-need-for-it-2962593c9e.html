Did I just spent 6 months building a product that no one wants even there is a need for it

by api_explorer

For the past few months, I've been building a product "Linden". Its a low latency deterministic layer for ai agents. Linden helps businesses verify AI-generated outputs before they lead to real-world actions. It evaluates AI outputs against expected schemas, trusted data, and business rules to determine whether an action should be allowed, flagged, regenerated, blocked, or sent for human review.

Linden helps businesses ensure their AI agents don't just produce convincing answers — they make decisions that align with actual business requirements, verified information, and configured limits.

I have tried cold email outreach that didn't work. Published a professional demo across multiple platforms, drove 0 signups. Now, i don't know if I just built something that serves no purpose. However, when i was building this, i did a research, looked at a lot of articles talking about the same thing. If this problem exists, then why no one willing to try it out?

    One thing about "the research says the problem exists": articles are evidence that a problem is talked about, not that it is budgeted. Verification of AI outputs gets bought the way insurance gets bought - after a scare, not after reading about risk. The companies that pay for it are the ones that already had an agent do something expensive: a wrong refund, a bad email to a client, a broken deployment.

    So the targeting question is not "who builds agents" but "who has been burned by one, visibly". Those incidents are public: postmortems, horror stories on X and HN, "our agent did X" threads. Reach out to those specific people with their incident as the opener. "Here is how Linden would have caught exactly this" beats any generic demo, because you are selling to a memory, not a hypothesis.

    That also explains the cold email result. To someone who has not had a failure yet, you are selling a tax. To someone who has, you are selling relief.

    Articles about a problem tell you that people find it interesting, not that someone has a budget for it this month. That gap is usually where the 0 signups come from.

    The people who will try a verification layer are the ones who already got burned: an agent that sent a wrong refund, emailed the wrong customer, or wrote bad data into a CRM. They don't search for "deterministic layer for AI agents", they search for the incident. So instead of a general demo, I'd look for teams who've posted about a specific failure (GitHub issues, agent framework Discords, postmortems on X) and offer to wire Linden into their setup for free, for that one failure.

    I'd also pick one use case and name it in the headline. "Stop your support agent from issuing refunds over $X without approval" is something a person recognizes as their problem. "Verify AI outputs against business rules" is something they nod at and close the tab.

    And cold email for an infra product works much better with a 2-line offer than a pitch: "I saw your post about X, I built something that would have blocked that, want me to set it up on your repo this week?"
