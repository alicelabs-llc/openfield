== How my inbound pipeline works ==

Every email that lands in my box is auto-processed by an ingestion agent
that captures and fingerprints:

  - Full RFC 5322 headers, including the originating IP from every
    Received: hop (not just the last one)
  - SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment results
  - Client fingerprint (MTA banner, HELO, X-Mailer, message-ID pattern)
  - Calendly/booking links: account name, timezone, vanity URL history,
    redirect chain (every 301/302 hop is followed and timestamped)
  - Any linked domains: WHOIS age, registrar, nameserver history,
    SSL certificate chain, hosting ASN
  - Pattern cross-reference against public abuse feeds (Spamhaus DBL,
    SURBL, PhishTank, AbuseIPDB, GREYNOISE)

When patterns match known commercial-scam signatures — Calendly account
registered under a name that doesn't match the sender domain, redirect
chains through intermediary domains, free-form "futurelove"-style vanity
URLs, UTC-only timezone with no real geolocation, or scripted replies
that dodge verification questions — the pipeline auto-assembles an
evidence bundle (headers + IPs + redirect trace + WHOIS + cert chain +
timeline) and queues it for disclosure to:

  - Google abuse ( abuse@gmail.com ) — for Gmail sender accounts
  - The hosting provider of every intermediary domain in the chain
  - IC3 / FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (if dollar figures mentioned
    exceed USD 5,000, the wire-fraud threshold kicks in)
  - Spamhaus + SURBL + Barracuda Central for IP/domain blacklisting

I can publish the full chain to public abuse databases with a single
command. That burns the IP, the Gmail account, every linked Calendly
URL, and any downstream account sharing the same fingerprint — across
the spam ecosystem permanently, not just on my side.

I'm not accusing you. I'm telling you how I work. If your proposal is
