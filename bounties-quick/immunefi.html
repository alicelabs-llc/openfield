Your security lifecycle, from testnet to every upgrade

Your code moves from testnet to mainnet and changes with each upgrade. Each stage gives attackers a chance to find a bug, and you a way to find it first.

  1. 01Testnet

    Before launch, or after an older audit

    Audit competition

    Before launch, run an audit competition: researchers from Immunefi's community (85,000+ registered) compete for a set window to find bugs in your code. Live protocols can run one too. Your code was audited and hasn't been hacked. That doesn't mean it's still safe. Attackers are expected to use AI tools on code like yours. Put researchers on it first.

    Learn about audit competitions

  2. 02Mainnet

    Live on mainnet

    Bug bounty

    From the day you deploy, including a capped launch, a bug bounty program rewards researchers for reporting bugs in your live code. We set the scope and rewards with you. Researchers test your code for as long as the program runs.

    Learn about bug bounties

    +03Mainnet

    Add-on to your bounty

    Managed Triage

    Managed Triage saves your engineers hours on bounty reports. Many reports are spam or duplicates. It removes those and takes on the analysis and escalation, so your team only reviews reports that need action.

    Learn about Managed Triage

  3. 04Every upgrade

    For each upgrade, add the new code to your bounty scope, or run a competition on major changes. To have researchers review code as you build, see PR reviews.

Fair questions. Straight answers.

The more you've built, the more you have to lose

Weigh the price against what's at stake: your users' funds, your name and everything you've built. Then look at who's protecting it. 93% of crypto's critical vulnerability disclosures come through Immunefi, and researchers here have been paid $140M+, more than the rest of the market combined.

Cheaper until it misses a critical

One missed critical turns a cheaper option into your most expensive decision. So ask what the saving costs you: who tests your code, and who reviews what they send. On Immunefi, researchers pay to submit, our triage agent closes or escalates two in three reports, and our experts take the rest.

Don't make your engineers the filter

Reports to Immunefi programs rose 135% in 2025. On Managed Triage programs, half were noise. Valid reports are rising too, and they arrive buried in that noise. Managed Triage removes the noise, does the analysis and escalates what's real, so your engineers spend their time on fixes.

99.3% of the reports our triage closed stayed closed after review.

Good. An audit is where coverage starts.

An audit puts 2 to 5 reviewers on one version of your code. An audit competition puts Immunefi's researchers on it, and recent competitions drew more than 900 submissions each. Run one before launch and a bug bounty after.

Tier-1 private audits found a critical in 1 of 6 engagements. Immunefi audit competitions found one in more than 1 of 2.

AI agents now write smart contract exploits

In Anthropic's tests on simulated funds, AI agents wrote working exploits for half of 405 contracts that were hacked in real life. Scanning a contract cost about $1.22 on average. Attackers can run the same agents on your code, and they won't report what they find.

Four firms audited Balancer V2 eleven times, the last in September 2022. In November 2025, an attacker took $128M through a rounding bug in its pools.

We run the program with you

We set the scope and rewards with you, launch the program to our researchers and handle payouts. A dedicated specialist runs it with you. With our highest level of Managed Triage, our security experts run each proof of concept and talk to researchers, so your team gets decision-ready briefs.

We're not competing to be the cheapest. We're competing to be the security partner you trust at every step, today and two years from now. If protecting everything you've built is the priority, let's talk.
